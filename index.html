01 / BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING

Make the prediction question precise.

The academic value is in a careful, bounded analysis—not in presenting a classifier as a decision-maker.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How well can machine-learning models classify the final outcome recorded in the Portuguese student dataset, and which observed features matter most to those predictions?

We compare four classifiers with a most-frequent baseline, assess each outcome separately, and inspect feature importance and target-excluded clusters.

A

SignificanceStudent attrition carries educational and social consequences; careful analysis can inform questions for future support research.

B

ContributionA transparent single-dataset workflow, with explicit target timing, leakage controls and class-specific evaluation.

C

BoundaryThe dataset contains results from both semesters. This study does not claim to predict dropout early enough to intervene.

  1. 01

    Load and verify the original file

    analysis.py reads data.csv with sep=";", cleans a possible BOM and surrounding whitespace from column names, then checks the expected target and feature columns. The source rows and labels remain unchanged.

  2. 02

    Check data quality

    The script counts missing cells and exact duplicate rows before modeling. This copy has 0 missing cells and 0 exact duplicate rows, so no rows are dropped and no values are imputed in the reported analysis.

  3. 03

    Inspect features and separate the target

    X holds 36 predictors and y keeps the string labels Dropout, Enrolled and Graduate. The analysis declares 17 coded categorical fields; the remaining 19 predictors are numeric. The target is never passed into feature encoding.

  4. 04

    Reserve the holdout before fitting

    A stratified split with test_size=0.20 and random_state=42 assigns 3,539 records to training and 885 to the untouched holdout. Five-fold stratified cross-validation runs within training only.

  5. 05

    Encode categories and prepare numeric inputs

    Model pipelines use most-frequent imputation followed by one-hot encoding for the 17 categorical fields, with unseen categories ignored. Numeric fields have median imputation defined; standardization is enabled for Logistic Regression and passed through for the tree models.

  6. 06

    Fit fold-safe, model-ready inputs

    Each pipeline model fits preprocessing on its fold's training records, then transforms validation records; the most-frequent baseline needs no feature transforms. After weighted-F1 selection, the selected estimator is fit on all training records and evaluated once on the untouched holdout.

RESEARCH DESIGN

Retrospective, quantitative classification.

The study classifies the final recorded outcome in one Portuguese higher-education dataset. It evaluates prediction on a held-out portion of this dataset; it does not test prospective intervention or causal effects.

IBM SPSS Modeler CRISP-DM guide ↗

EXPERIMENTAL WORKFLOW

Split, compare, then evaluate once.

A stratified 80/20 split reserves 885 records as holdout and leaves 3,539 for training. Five-fold stratified cross-validation runs only on training records. Pipelines fit their preprocessing within each fold; the holdout is not used for model selection.

MODEL SELECTION

Weighted F1 chooses the candidate.

Five configurations share the same training partition and folds. The highest mean training-only cross-validation weighted F1 selects the model. No resampling, threshold search or holdout-based tuning is performed.

EVALUATION APPROACH

Keep cross-validation and holdout distinct.

Cross-validation reports fold means and standard deviations for accuracy, weighted F1, macro F1 and macro one-vs-rest ROC-AUC. The selected model’s untouched holdout reports accuracy, weighted precision and recall, weighted and macro F1, macro OvR ROC-AUC, per-class scores and a confusion matrix.

SUPPLEMENTARY ANALYSES

Interpretation remains descriptive.

A separate 300-tree Random Forest estimates five-repeat permutation importance on the holdout. K-Means excludes the target and compares k=2–6 with silhouette scores. Neither analysis establishes causes or validated student types.

REPRODUCIBILITY

One script regenerates the outputs.

analysis.py uses random state 42 and the pinned environment in requirements.txt. It writes the JSON used by the website, the local-file data companion and a text report. Detailed transformations remain in Preparation.

07 / EVALUATION

Interpret the scores in the context of their errors.

Evaluation explains what the results support, how model behavior differs across outcomes, and where this experiment cannot generalize.

MODEL STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES

Compare against the baseline and each other.

Model comparisons are calculated from training-only cross-validation.

WEIGHTED AND MACRO METRICS

Class balance changes the summary.

Weighted metrics reflect class support; macro F1 gives each outcome equal weight.

CLASS-SPECIFIC ERRORS

Overall performance hides uneven recall.

Read the per-class report and confusion matrix together to see which outcomes are most often confused.

IMPORTANCE AND CLUSTERING

Descriptive evidence is not explanation.

Permutation importance measures reliance of one fitted Random Forest and can shift when predictors are correlated. K-Means outcome shares describe groups after an unsupervised fit; neither analysis establishes why a student leaves or proves natural student types.

LIMITATIONS AND RELIABILITY

This is a random stratified holdout from one historical dataset, not a later cohort, external institution or prospective study. Second-semester features make the task retrospective. The experiment does not establish calibration, fairness, intervention value or deployment readiness; performance may change across time and populations.

08 / IMPLICATIONS

Translate evidence into responsible next research.

This is not an operational model or prospective evaluation. Any future support use requires new validation, explicit timing and human oversight.

01

Define the prediction time.

Future early-warning research should set a prediction landmark and exclude information recorded after it.

02

Test beyond this cohort.

Validate on later and external student cohorts before considering use in another institution or time period.

03

Study support outcomes.

Work with students and educators to assess calibration, fairness and whether an intervention helps.

04

Keep people accountable.

Any future predictions should support human-reviewed services and never restrict educational opportunity.
