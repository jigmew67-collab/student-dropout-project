THE PROBLEM

From student records to earlier insight.

“

Research question

How do five model configurations—four classification models and one majority-class baseline—perform within two distinct prediction tasks: final Dropout, Enrolled and Graduate outcomes in the UCI data, and subsequent withdrawal by day 28 in OULAD? Which features and exploratory student profiles are associated with each outcome?

Why it matters

Student attrition has academic, social and economic consequences. Predictive analytics can help institutions recognize patterns associated with risk and support more timely, targeted intervention—but predictions should support human decision-making rather than replace it.

01

Compare four classifiers and a majority-class baseline separately within each dataset.

02

Evaluate untouched holdouts with class-sensitive metrics, confusion matrices and ROC-AUC where appropriate.

03

Interpret predictors and explore target-free student clusters without claiming causes.

Data quality and preparation

The raw OULAD tables and day-28 analysis are audited separately. The included UCI file has no missing values. OULAD missing values are measured from the generated analysis output.

OULAD missing values are not all the same: some are unavailable assessment measurements, some are absent demographic categories, and some raw-table nulls are structural or outside the model feature set. Assessment means are median-imputed and categorical gaps are represented as Unknown within training-fold pipelines; no missing value is automatically replaced by zero. Zero VLE click aggregates denote no qualifying recorded clicks; the early count is the number of non-missing scores.

Available predictors cover demographics, enrollment, module and presentation design, early assessment score/count signals and VLE click totals by activity type. Final outcomes, student IDs, unregistration dates and post-cutoff activity are excluded from predictors. Fail is not treated as dropout; only later Withdrawn records are positive. Student identifiers group every registration for the same student in model evaluation.

Controlled missing-value experiments

All variants use the original train/test splits and training-only cross-validation. Numerical values are median-imputed within each fold; categorical gaps are handled as explicit Unknown values for OULAD. The comparison tests whether fitting this handling inside each fold, and adding missingness indicators, changes performance. Holdout metrics are reported but not used to select the preprocessing variant.

Why the datasets stay separate

UCI predicts three end-of-study labels using 36 institutional features. OULAD predicts subsequent withdrawal using day-28 online learning and enrollment signals. Different countries, populations, feature timing and label definitions mean the scores are descriptive within each experiment, not a direct country-level comparison.

OULAD has no duplicate registration keys; seven rows share identical predictors after anonymized student identifiers are excluded.

PIPELINE 1 · DATA ACQUISITION

Two-source preparation

  1. Read UCI CSV and original OULAD tables
  2. Validate keys, labels, missingness and duplicates
  3. UCI: preserve all 36 features; encode 17 categories inside model pipeline
  4. OULAD: join tables; aggregate activity and eligible assessment signals through day 28
  5. Drop IDs and final outcome fields from predictors; impute and encode in-fold

PIPELINE 2 · TRAINING & EVALUATION

Independent experiments

  1. UCI: stratified 80/20 holdout; five-fold stratified CV on training records
  2. OULAD: five-fold stratified-group split; student IDs cannot cross partitions
  3. Compare baseline and four fixed classifiers; select on CV weighted F1
  4. Evaluate selected model once on holdout; report per-class scores and confusion matrix
  5. Rank predictors by holdout permutation importance; cluster without target features
Prediction timing and interpretation

UCI includes first- and second-semester results and is therefore a retrospective final-outcome classification experiment, not an early-warning model. OULAD is a day-28 withdrawal experiment. Feature importance describes predictive association; clustering is exploratory and neither proves causation.

Accuracy

The share of all predictions that are correct.

Precision

Of the students predicted as a class, the share actually in that class.

Recall

Of the students truly in a class, the share the model identifies.

Weighted F1

Balances precision and recall, then weights each class by its size.

Confusion matrix

Shows exactly which true classes are confused with which predictions.

Macro OvR ROC-AUC

Measures probability ranking for each class versus the rest, then averages all three classes equally.

WHAT THE RESULTS MEAN

The useful signal is mostly academic—but prediction is not intervention.

All four trained models beat the majority-class baseline. Histogram Gradient Boosting achieved the highest mean training-CV weighted F1. On the untouched holdout, Enrolled remained the hardest class: only 68 of 159 cases were identified, while 41 were predicted as Dropout and 50 as Graduate. Its 42.77% recall is much lower than Dropout at 72.18% and Graduate at 89.14%.

The Enrolled group is also the smallest class, and its observed outcomes overlap with both non-completion and graduation patterns. This is a plausible interpretation of the confusion pattern, not a causal finding; class-weighting and timing experiments are needed to test it.

These associations should not be treated as causal explanations or automatic decisions about individual students. A real early-warning system should be validated on local data and used with human oversight.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Useful evidence, with clear boundaries.

01

Class imbalance

Both tasks are imbalanced. UCI Enrolled recall is 42.77%, and OULAD Withdrawn recall is 5.58% at the default threshold. Accuracy alone is insufficient; future threshold and class-sensitive experiments should select settings using training data only.

02

Generalizability

UCI represents one Portuguese institution; OULAD represents seven historical UK online modules. Neither result guarantees performance at other institutions, courses, or future cohorts.

03

Prediction timing

UCI includes both semesters and is retrospective. OULAD predicts subsequent withdrawal only among registrations active at day 28. These are different prediction times and cannot be interpreted as a direct country comparison.

04

Responsible improvement

Future work should use nested tuning when optimizing models, evaluate probability calibration and fairness, and test intervention value with human oversight rather than treating predictions as decisions.
