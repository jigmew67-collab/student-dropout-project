THE PROBLEM

From student records to earlier insight.

“

Research question

How effectively can machine-learning models predict whether a student will Drop Out, remain Enrolled, or Graduate using academic, demographic and socioeconomic information?

Why it matters

Student attrition has academic, social and economic consequences. Predictive analytics can help institutions recognize patterns associated with risk and support more timely, targeted intervention—but predictions should support human decision-making rather than replace it.

TARGET DISTRIBUTION

The classes are imbalanced: Graduate is the largest class, while Enrolled is the smallest. This is why we report weighted F1 alongside accuracy.

Academic

Admission grade, previous qualification, curricular units, evaluations, approved units and semester grades.

Demographic

Age at enrollment, gender, nationality, marital status, international and displaced status.

Socioeconomic

Scholarship, debtor status, tuition-fee status, parental qualification and occupation.

Macroeconomic

Unemployment rate, inflation rate and GDP.

WHAT THE RESULTS MEAN

The useful signal is mostly academic—but prediction is not intervention.

All trained models beat the majority-class baseline. Gradient Boosting produced the strongest average cross-validation result, while the holdout confusion matrix shows that Enrolled students are harder to classify cleanly. A Random Forest importance check ranked second-semester approved units, first-semester approved units and semester grades among the strongest predictors.

These associations should not be treated as causal explanations or automatic decisions about individual students. A real early-warning system should be validated on local data and used with human oversight.
